## 1. How the real Language Model behaves in ToolShift situations

Before any controller existed, the real model answered 24 situations of each kind built from **training** identities (`characterize.py`). It reads plain names and alias notes almost perfectly, does not guess unknown aliases or nonexistent tanks (it abstains), uses a distilled dynamics note in most cases, plans correctly from the World Model's consequence table or consultation, and **cannot infer changed tool behaviour from raw observation notes** (0 of 24). That last finding means Language Model adaptation alone cannot repair a dynamics shift on this model: only knowledge that passes through the World Model works. Its token confidence separates acting (about -0.0003) from abstaining (about -0.4) and carries no information about whether an action is right. These rates parameterize the simulation surrogate, with a uniform-prior posterior mean in place of each raw proportion.

## 2. World Model learner

Decision D-WM. The same transition sequences (version A, a shifted version B, then A again) were given to four learners with context timing supplied, isolating the learner from the controller. Context separation is what prevents forgetting whichever learner is inside it; the Bayesian learner is what makes adaptation few-shot and exact; on capped dynamics outside its basis it is wrong but says so (high coverage of its own intervals). The MLP arms were trained with 300 Adam steps per update after a 30-step version was found unfairly weak.

## 4. The selected Erudition Model

The 1,259,700-parameter transformer (seed 1) was selected because it had the lowest joint-arm failure among architectures with at least one million parameters.

**Deviation from the stated rule.** The rule (lowest failure, ties within 0.01 to the smaller model) selects the 1,020,692-parameter GRU: its joint failure, 0.3329, is 0.0098 from the transformer's 0.3231, which is inside the tie band. The implementer applied the tie clause only to the 109K model and missed the GRU. The error was found by an independent review after confirmation had started with the transformer. Restarting would have spent a second confirmation attempt, so the transformer remains the confirmed artifact, and this deviation is part of the record. The rule itself was written to a working note outside Git before the first sweep; its timing is evidenced by that file's timestamp, not by Git history. **The 109K transformer is within the declared 0.01 tie band of it, so on this environment the owner's one-million-parameter floor, not the evidence, sets the size.** The 4.98M transformer was no better. Two further seeds of the selected architecture landed on either side of the rule-based heuristic in simulation (table above): **the learned controller's simulated advantage over the rules is within seed variance**. Seed 1 was not the worst of the three, so by the rule it is the artifact. It was committed (`model/erudition.pt`) before any real-model Erudition run.

Weights SHA-256 `c263650cb7c1af7dc91b905fff583e62448fe04064bf44f3ec2ebb881430f94f`; 1,259,700 trainable parameters; decision margin 0.01; trained 20 epochs on 3200 simulated streams (132,955 examples) in 892 s on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060.

## 5. Real-model development

Six development streams, one per family, on the real gpt-oss-20b with the frozen Erudition Model and the controls; every run re-executed by `recompute` before it is counted. The confirmation contracts and their margins (`tools/write_aaa_erudition_freeze.py`, first committed at `fca64f3`) were fixed **after** the frozen and rule-based development runs had been made, and **before** any real-model Erudition run. They are therefore not blind to the reference arms' development results.

What it shows:

- **Adaptation transfers to the real model.** Joint adaptation by the Erudition Model more than halves task failure against never adapting, with no measurable damage to tasks no shift touched.
- **Both components matter, through different families.** Language Model adaptation alone repairs the language families and cannot repair a dynamics shift on this model (the characterization predicted it: GPT-OSS does not infer tool behaviour from raw observations). World Model adaptation alone repairs dynamics shifts through the revision and consultation turns and nothing else. Joint adaptation covers both, and beats either single-component arm.
- **The joint-shift family is hard for every controller.** When aliases and dynamics change together, the Language Model abstains until aliases are learned, so the World Model receives no executed calls to learn from; that sequential dependency does not finish inside the shift window before the original world returns. Neither the Erudition Model nor the rules recover much there. Joint adaptation in this family is necessary but, at this timescale, not sufficient.
- **Learned control versus rules.** On failure the Erudition Model is close to the rule set (slightly worse here, inside six-stream noise). In the joint arm on these development streams it held no wrong alias note where the rules did. It was not immune: in the LM-only arm it was poisoned on a similar share of steps, and on the attack streams in about two-thirds of the rules' share (section 6). It spent well under half the rules' gate and extraction calls.
- **A visible controller weakness.** The Erudition Model issues many requests that do nothing (rollbacks with nothing to roll back, recalls with one stored context). They cost no model calls, but a no-op joint recall starts a cooldown that can delay a useful request, and they show that its action values are poorly separated where waiting and a no-op are equivalent in training.

## 8. Failures, defects and what they taught

Every item below happened in this phase and is kept on record. Superseded outputs are retained outside the repository under the workspace's data root, not reported as results.

1. **The first real-model pilot (training stream 0) barely beat never adapting.** The record showed three causes:
   - the rule set re-requested a rejected mechanism every cooldown and never saw a World Model signal while the model abstained;
   - the Language Model gate scored alias candidates on corrections they did not touch;
   - the World Model was consulted only after an `act` proposal, although GPT-OSS correctly abstains when the stale manual makes a target unreachable.

   Repairs: in-scope alias replay (SERAC's lesson), a World Model consultation after abstentions offered identically to every arm, and a stronger rule set (documented in its docstring as revised in development).
2. **A single disagreeing World Model prediction made GPT-OSS abstain;** a table of predicted consequences for every `n` made it plan correctly. The integration contract uses the table.
3. **The surrogate leaked correctness into a controller input.** An independent read-only code review before handoff found that the simulated model's confidence was drawn from correctness-conditioned distributions, while the real model is equally confident whether right or wrong (characterization: about -0.0003 for both). Every simulated dataset and every model trained on it was discarded and regenerated with confidence conditioned on proposal kind only, and the surrogate's hidden knowledge was reduced to which tank a request refers to. The same review found twelve further defects, all repaired before any confirmation identity existed:
   - metrics that could not fail, or were biased against one arm;
   - recomputation that trusted most stored fields;
   - confirmation contracts not tied to the freeze, and missing runs not detected;
   - admission flags that bypassed the freeze, and an evidence checker that failed open;
   - weak correction verification, so a real tank name could be "corrected" into another;
   - an in-sample gate for experience notes;
   - a canary rollback that could be partial and could resurrect cleared canaries;
   - nondeterministic run summaries;
   - documentation drift.
4. **Metrics that were tautologies.** `missed_adaptation` first counted every deficiency interval, and the first `unresolved_deficiency` was 1 by construction. Both were caught by constructed-run tests that require each metric to take its meaningful values, and both were redefined before any reported result.
5. **Failure injection caught a test gap.** The deliberate "World Model gate accepts any candidate" break survived the suite until a direct gain test was added. All eleven injected breaks are now caught.
6. **The machine went down twice** while heavy simulation or training ran beside the resident model. Phases are now strictly separated; interrupted outputs were trimmed and resumed, with byte-reproducibility verified.
7. **The runtime build disappeared** in an outside storage clean-up and was rebuilt from the same commit and recipe; its parsed actions matched the recorded runtime on 30/30 probes. Cached responses were also compacted after it emerged that alternative-token data made each call about 50 KB.
8. **The learned controller issues no-op requests** (rollbacks with nothing to roll back, recalls with one context), and **no controller recovers much in the joint-shift family** (section 5). Both are reported as limitations, not tuned away after development.
